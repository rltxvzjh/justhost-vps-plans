## Two things in the cheap tiers that the plan cards skip

### The sub-$2 entry has no IPv4 address

The $1.60 line is IPv6-only. Most of the internet is reachable over IPv6 now, but plenty of the plumbing isn't: older monitoring endpoints, some payment and mail APIs, a fair number of corporate VPNs. If you want a normal IPv4 address, the real floor is the $2.60 HDD VPS Start.

And at that floor, Sirius beats it outright: $2.25 gets you twice the RAM, four times the storage and NVMe instead of a spinning disk. For a first VPS the $1.60 line looks like the obvious saving and usually isn't.

### "300 Mbit" comes with an asterisk

Every port figure on the special line is marked **fair-share**. The page does the arithmetic for you in the same line: 300 Mbit works out to 93+ TB of monthly transfer, 500 Mbit to 155+ TB, 750 Mbit to 232+ TB. Traffic is unmetered, so you aren't billed for going over.

Fair-share means the throughput is shared with other tenants on the same machine rather than guaranteed to you. For a website, a bot, a proxy or a backup target, that's fine and the volume ceiling never comes into play. If you're planning sustained saturation of a gigabit link, or you're reselling bandwidth, this is not the product for that, and no amount of unmetered traffic changes it.

## What an independent test of the São Paulo machine found

A review published in March 2026 ran benchmarks on a JustHost Brazil VPS built on an E5-2699v4 host. The useful numbers:

- **Geekbench 6: 705 single-core, 1,822 multi-core.** Modest. Fine for a web stack, a bot or a light database, not fine for CPU-bound compilation or heavy transcoding.
- **Disk I/O around 509 MB/s, 4K read/write near 82 MB/s each, roughly 20.6K IOPS.** Sane NVMe numbers for the price bracket.
- **Latency to Asia was poor**, and the reviewer's own conclusion was that the Brazil location suits Brazilian users or a transit setup, not direct traffic from East Asia.
- **The IP unlocked TikTok Brazil, ChatGPT, Claude, Disney+ and Netflix, but not Gemini**, and that particular VM had no IPv6.

Read that as one machine in one location in March 2026, not a verdict on the whole catalogue. The latency finding is the one that generalises: pick the region closest to the people hitting the server, and use the one-click relocation if you get it wrong.
